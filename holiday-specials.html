The British TV Hub Holiday Guide: Specials Collection

Some Christmas specials are worth returning to every year, long after their first broadcast. This is a collection of the recurring British TV traditions that show up every December — not this year's new premieres, but the reliable, rewatchable ones. A Tradition Worth Keeping

These are the British shows that have made a Christmas special a genuine annual habit — the kind of thing you can put on every December regardless of what's new. This list only includes traditions with a real, ongoing track record, not one-off specials being treated as if they're annual.

1. Death in Paradise: Christmas Specials

Annual Tradition

Death in Paradise: Christmas Specials

Annual since 2011 · BBC · BritBox

Every December, the Honoré police department gets one more festive murder to solve on the fictional Caribbean island of Saint Marie. It's been a fixture of British Christmas TV since the series began, with a new detective era or two along the way but the same sun-drenched cozy mystery formula.

What Readers Ask Most

What British shows have a Christmas special every year?

Death in Paradise and its spin-off Beyond Paradise have both aired a Christmas special annually since debuting. Call the Midwife has released one every year since 2012. Sherlock's special was a one-off rather than an annual tradition.

Where can I watch past Call the Midwife Christmas specials?

PBS periodically streams a run of past holiday specials in the lead-up to the new Christmas special, available through PBS.org, the PBS app, and PBS Passport for members. Availability rotates, so check PBS's current schedule.

Is this year's new Call the Midwife special included here?

No — this collection covers the recurring, evergreen specials from these shows. For 2026's brand-new specials, including Call the Midwife: Sisters in Arms, see British TV Hub's Holiday Guide: What to Watch.

Do I need to watch the full series before these specials?

Death in Paradise, Beyond Paradise, and Call the Midwife specials are generally accessible to newcomers, with self-contained mysteries or seasonal storylines. Sherlock: The Abominable Bride works best with some familiarity with the main series, since it plays with the show's modern-day format.
