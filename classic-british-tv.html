Classic British TV

Long before Downton Abbey and modern detective drama, these were the shows that shaped British television — the wartime mysteries, below-stairs sagas, literary adaptations, and landmark police dramas that helped shape generations of British television.

What Counts as a Classic?

This is the broader home for older British television — not just detective drama, but the period pieces, wartime mysteries, and literary adaptations that laid the groundwork for everything that came after. Looking specifically for classic detective shows? See the dedicated guide.

1. Foyle's War

WWII Mystery

Foyle's War

2002–2015 · ITV · Acorn TV

Michael Kitchen plays Detective Chief Superintendent Christopher Foyle, investigating crime on England's south coast while World War II rages around him. Created by Anthony Horowitz — the same writer behind Magpie Murders, Moonflower Murders, and Marble Hall Murders — the show wove real wartime events and moral dilemmas into each case, across nine series.

2. Upstairs, Downstairs

Downton Abbey's Predecessor

Upstairs, Downstairs

1971–1975 · ITV · BritBox

Created by Jean Marsh and Eileen Atkins, this landmark drama follows the wealthy Bellamy family and their household staff at 165 Eaton Place in Belgravia, spanning three decades of social change from 1903 to 1930. Often cited as the spiritual predecessor to Downton Abbey, the original series ran five series and won the BAFTA for Best Drama Series.

5. The Forsyte Saga

The Original British Miniseries

The Forsyte Saga

1967 original · BBC2 · Not currently streaming in the US

Adapted from the celebrated Forsyte novels by Nobel Prize-winning author John Galsworthy, the 1967 original starred Eric Porter and Susan Hampshire and became a television phenomenon in both the UK and the US. The original is not currently streaming in the US. Viewers interested in a newer interpretation can find the 2026 series The Forsytes on PBS Passport.

7. Jonathan Creek

Impossible-Crime Mystery

Jonathan Creek

1997–2016 · BBC One · BritBox

Alan Davies plays Jonathan Creek, a reclusive magic-trick designer whose expertise in illusion makes him uniquely suited to solving seemingly impossible crimes. Created and written by David Renwick, initially paired with crime writer Maddy Magellan (Caroline Quentin) across five series and several specials.

8. The Bill

Landmark Police Procedural

The Bill

1984–2010 · ITV · Not currently streaming

Following the uniformed officers and detectives of the fictional Sun Hill police station in East London, The Bill ran from 1984 to 2010 and produced more than 2,400 episodes, making it one of Britain's longest-running police dramas. The series is not currently available through a major US subscription streaming service, although DVD editions can still be found.

What Readers Ask Most

What's the difference between this page and Classic British Detective Shows?

Classic British Detective Shows focuses specifically on 70s-90s detective drama. This page is broader, covering classic British television generally — period drama, WWII-era mystery, and the shows that laid the groundwork for everything from Downton Abbey to modern detective drama.

Is Foyle's War based on real events?

The overarching WWII history is real, and creator Anthony Horowitz wove real wartime events and issues into each episode's fictional case, but Foyle and his investigations themselves are fictional.

Is the original Upstairs, Downstairs related to Downton Abbey?

Not directly, but it's often considered Downton Abbey's spiritual predecessor — both share the upstairs/downstairs structure of a wealthy family and their servants, and Julian Fellowes has cited it as an influence.

Where can I find the classic Christie adaptations?

British TV Hub has full guides to both Agatha Christie's Poirot and Miss Marple, plus a complete Agatha Christie TV adaptations guide covering her wider body of work.

Why aren't The Forsyte Saga and The Bill listed as streaming?

The original 1967 Forsyte Saga is not currently available through major US streaming services, although it can be found on DVD. A newer interpretation, the 2026 adaptation The Forsytes, is available on PBS Passport. The Bill is also not currently available through a major US subscription service, though DVD editions can still be found.
