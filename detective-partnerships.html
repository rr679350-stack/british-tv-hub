British Detective Partnerships

Some of the best British crime drama isn't really about the crime at all — it's about the pairing. The mentor and the protege, the outsider and the local, the instinct and the method. These are the detective duos that keep fans coming back, and where to stream each one in the US.

What Makes a Great Detective Partnership?

The best British detective duos work because the two halves genuinely complement each other — instinct paired with method, experience paired with youth, an insider paired with an outsider. The friction (or the lack of it) is often more compelling than the mystery itself.

What Readers Ask Most

What are the best British detective partnerships?

Endeavour and Thursday, Lewis and Hathaway, Hardy and Miller from Broadchurch, and Cassie and Sunny from Unforgotten are among the most consistently praised. Vera's partnerships with her sergeants and Grantchester's vicar-and-inspector pairing are also fan favourites.

What's the difference between Endeavour and Thursday, and Lewis and Hathaway?

They're both set in the Morse universe, but the dynamic is reversed: in Endeavour, young Morse is the junior partner learning from DI Fred Thursday. In Lewis, Morse's own former sergeant has become the senior figure, grounding his academic junior partner, DS Hathaway.

Which of these detective partnerships are on BritBox?

Endeavour, Lewis, Vera, McDonald & Dodds, and Unforgotten are all available on BritBox in the US. See the BritBox Guide for the full catalogue.

Which of these detective partnerships are on PBS Masterpiece?

Endeavour, Lewis, Broadchurch, Unforgotten, and Grantchester have all aired as part of PBS Masterpiece, and are available with PBS Passport.

What should I watch after Vera?

Unforgotten and Broadchurch both share Vera's character-driven, slow-burn approach to crime drama. For a similarly grounded partnership dynamic, Lewis and Hathaway is also a strong next watch.

Are any of these detective partnerships based on books?

Vera is based on Ann Cleeves' novels, and Grantchester is based on James Runcie's Grantchester Mysteries books. The others were written directly for television.
