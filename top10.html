The Best British Police Procedurals

From the Oxford cobblestones of Inspector Morse to the Yorkshire moors of Happy Valley — the definitive guide to British police drama, classics and new favourites alike, with where to watch each one in the US.

No one does the police procedural quite like the British. From the slow-burn intelligence of Inspector Morse to the breathless tension of Line of Duty, British police procedurals have spent four decades proving that the genre can be as much about character and place as it is about the crime. The best of them trust the viewer to keep up — and reward you when you do.

The Classics That Started It All

These are the shows that invented the genre. Foundational, essential, and every one of them still completely gripping today.

Classic

Inspector Morse

1987–2000 · ITV · BritBox

John Thaw's Morse — Oxford don, opera lover, crossword obsessive — remains the most fully realised detective in British TV history. Every feature-length episode is a masterclass in character, atmosphere, and plotting. The entire Morse universe flows from this one extraordinary show.

Prime Suspect

1991–2006 · ITV · BritBox

Helen Mirren as DCI Jane Tennison — brilliant, driven, and fighting institutional sexism at every rank of the Met while hunting serial killers. Seven feature-length films over fifteen years, every one outstanding. A landmark not just in crime drama but in what television can do with a character over time.

Agatha Christie's Poirot

1989–2013 · ITV · BritBox

David Suchet spent 25 years perfecting Hercule Poirot — every Agatha Christie novel adapted, culminating in a finale that left the nation genuinely moved. For many fans, it's the definitive Christie adaptation.

Classic

The Bill

1983–2010 · ITV · Amazon Video

Twenty-seven years at Sun Hill police station — the longest-running British police procedural ever made. Before The Bill, TV coppers were tidy figures of authority. This gave them complicated marriages, drinking problems, and moral blind spots. Gritty, groundbreaking, and still compelling today.

Classic

Endeavour

2012–2023 · ITV · BritBox

The Morse prequel that had no right to be this good. Shaun Evans plays the young Endeavour Morse in 1960s Oxford, and across nine series the show builds its own identity while quietly laying every piece of groundwork for the beloved detective he becomes. The period detail is exquisite and the final series is a genuinely moving farewell to the whole Morse universe.

The Best of Modern Police Drama

British police procedurals didn't peak in the eighties — the golden age is still happening. These are the modern shows the community won't stop talking about.

Essential

Happy Valley

2014–2023 · BBC · BritBox

Sarah Lancashire as Sergeant Catherine Cawood — a Yorkshire officer holding her fractured family together while facing down a psychopath. Raw, powerful, and utterly gripping from first episode to last. The final series left fans genuinely bereft. Start here if you've never watched British crime drama before.

Line of Duty

2012–2021 · BBC · BritBox

AC-12, an anti-corruption unit, investigates bent coppers from within the force. The most compulsive crime series ever made in Britain — the question of who "H" is kept the entire country debating for years. Watch it with someone so you can argue about theories together.

Luther

2010–2019 · BBC · BritBox

Idris Elba as DCI John Luther — a brilliant detective whose empathy for the killers he hunts is exactly what makes him dangerous. Darker and more operatic than most British procedurals, it's a psychological thriller as much as a police show. Only twenty episodes across five series, which makes it the perfect weekend binge.

Essential

Broadchurch

2013–2017 · ITV · PBS / Free on Pluto TV

David Tennant and Olivia Colman investigate the death of an eleven-year-old boy in a small Dorset seaside town — and watch the community tear itself apart under the strain. The first series is one of the finest things British television has ever produced: a masterclass in grief, suspicion, and the cost of a single crime on everyone it touches. Best of all for US viewers, it streams free on PBS and Pluto TV.

New Favourite

Blue Lights

2023–present · BBC · BBC iPlayer (UK)

The freshest show on this list and one of the best in years. Blue Lights follows rookie police officers on their first year on the beat in present-day Belfast, where ordinary policing is anything but. It's tense, humane, and utterly authentic — the writers grew up there and it shows. The good news for US viewers: it's a BAFTA-winning sensation. The catch: it streams on BBC iPlayer, which you'll need a VPN to reach from the States.
