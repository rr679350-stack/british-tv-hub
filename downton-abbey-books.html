If You Love Downton Abbey, Read These Books

Here's the honest answer: Downton Abbey isn't based on a novel. It's an original screenplay by Julian Fellowes. But that doesn't mean there's nothing to read next.

Historical Drama · Some links may be affiliate links
The Official Books

The Downton Abbey Companion Books

Written by Jessica Fellowes — Julian Fellowes' niece — these are behind-the-scenes companions, not novelisations of the show's plot.

Titles like The World of Downton Abbey and Downton Abbey: A Celebration take you behind the scenes with cast interviews, episode guides, and the real social history behind the Crawleys' world. They're a treat for devoted fans, but worth knowing upfront: they're nonfiction companions, not a novel version of the story.
What to Read Instead

Fiction That Captures the Same World

Same era, same country-house atmosphere, same upstairs-downstairs tension — real novels worth your time.

01
By Julian Fellowes' Niece

The Mitford Murders — Jessica Fellowes

The same author who wrote the official Downton companions also writes original fiction: a Golden Age-style mystery series set among the real-life Mitford sisters in 1920s England, based on an actual unsolved murder.

Start Reading →
02
Booker Prize & Nobel Prize Winner

The Remains of the Day — Kazuo Ishiguro

A butler's reflections on a lifetime of service in a great English country house between the wars. Quieter and more literary than Downton, but the same world of duty, class, and the line between upstairs and downstairs.

Start Reading →
03
The Classic

The Forsyte Saga — John Galsworthy

A sweeping multi-generational saga of an upper-middle-class English family, first published in 1906. If Downton's family drama is what draws you in, this is the classic that set the template.
