Sunday Nights, Circa 1995

Remember when Sunday evening meant a pot of tea, the lamps on and a gentle drama to see the weekend out? These five favourites from the mid’90s are the very picture of that era, and every one of them is waiting for you on BritBox. Plus one hidden gem.

Pick No. 01

Heartbeat

Nick Berry stars as PC Nick Rowan, a young London policeman who settles into the North Yorkshire village of Aidensfield in the 1960s. Rolling moors, friendly faces and a soundtrack of ’60s hits make it one of the most comforting shows ever made.

Watch on BritBox →

Pick No. 02

Ballykissangel

Stephen Tompkinson plays Father Peter Clifford, an English priest who finds himself in a small Irish village full of big personalities, with the local pub at the heart of it all. Warm, funny and wonderfully romantic, it is a lovely trip back in time.

Watch on BritBox →

Pick No. 03

Hamish Macbeth

Robert Carlyle is the laid back village policeman of Lochdubh in the Scottish Highlands, who would much rather keep the peace his own way, with his little dog Wee Jock always at his side. Quirky, charming and full of glorious Highland scenery.

Watch on BritBox →

Pick No. 04

Pie in the Sky

Richard Griffiths plays Henry Crabbe, a detective who would far rather be in the kitchen of his own restaurant, Pie in the Sky. Every episode mixes a gentle case with plenty of good food, and it is as satisfying as a proper Sunday dinner.

Watch on BritBox →

Pick No. 05

Cadfael

Derek Jacobi plays Brother Cadfael, a 12th century monk and herbalist at Shrewsbury Abbey whose wisdom and patience help him untangle the puzzles that find their way to the abbey gates. Based on the beloved Ellis Peters novels, it is calm, thoughtful and quietly absorbing.

Watch on BritBox →

Hidden Gem

Hetty Wainthropp Investigates

Patricia Routledge plays Hetty, a Lancashire pensioner who decides retirement is far too quiet and sets herself up as a private detective, with young Geoffrey (Dominic Monaghan) as her trusty assistant. Cheerful, kind hearted and much too often overlooked.

Watch on BritBox →
