Final Bows

Some shows know exactly when to stop, and that is its own kind of gift. This week’s five all took their final bow with grace, from Grantchester’s farewell season to a few other endings worth revisiting, plus one hidden gem.

Pick No. 01

Grantchester

The vicar-detective drama wrapped its story with the warmth and heartbreak it always balanced so well. If you have been following Will Davenport and Geordie Keating all these years, the finale is the send-off they earned.

Watch on PBS Masterpiece →

Pick No. 02

Endeavour

The young-Morse prequel ended its run exactly where it needed to, closing the gap to the Oxford detective we already knew. A rare finale that satisfies both as a mystery and as a goodbye.

Watch on PBS Masterpiece →

Pick No. 03

Inspector Morse

The original. John Thaw’s Morse went out on his own terms in a finale that is still talked about decades later. If you have only met him through Endeavour, this is where the story completes itself.

Watch on BritBox →

Pick No. 04

Last Tango in Halifax

Two families, reunited by a late-in-life romance between childhood sweethearts, got a finale as funny and as tender as the series always was. A proper ending for a show that never stopped surprising people.

Watch on BritBox →

Pick No. 05

Foyle’s War

A wartime detective series that quietly became one of the best mysteries British TV ever made, and closed its run with the same careful integrity it started with. Worth revisiting start to finish.

Watch on PBS →

Hidden Gem

Wolf Hall

Mark Rylance’s Thomas Cromwell got his send-off in The Mirror and the Light, a quiet, devastating conclusion to one of the finest performances British television has produced. Not a cozy watch, but an unforgettable one.

Watch on PBS Masterpiece →
