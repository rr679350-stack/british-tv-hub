Christie and the Supernatural

Agatha Christie built her reputation on rational solutions, but she borrowed the trappings of the ghost story again and again: séances, curses, witchcraft, isolated houses. In most of these books the spooky atmosphere is a smokescreen for a very human killer, which is what makes them such good October reading. Two short-story collections at the end of this list go further and are genuinely supernatural.

The Halloween Essentials

The two Christie novels that lean hardest into October: one set at an actual Halloween party, one built around a trio of self-proclaimed witches.

Halloween Essential

Hallowe'en Party

Novel · 1969 · Hercule Poirot & Ariadne Oliver

At a children's Halloween party in the village of Woodleigh Common, a girl boasts that she once witnessed a murder, and before the night is over she is found drowned in the apple-bobbing tub. Ariadne Oliver calls in Poirot, and between the snapdragon and the party games, this is Christie's most obvious October read.

Séances and Spiritualists

Table-turning and ghostly glows: two cases where the spirit world seems to have something to say about murder.

Séance

The Sittaford Mystery

Novel · 1931 · Emily Trefusis & Inspector Narracott

On a snowbound evening on Dartmoor, a game of table-turning at Sittaford House spells out a message: Captain Trevelyan is dead. Six miles away in Exhampton, he is found murdered, and Emily Trefusis sets out to clear her fiancé.

Curses, Witches and Isolated Places

No séances here, just local legends, village whispers of black magic, and one island nobody leaves.

Gothic

Endless Night

Novel · 1967 · Narrated by Michael Rogers

Michael Rogers falls for a piece of land called Gipsy's Acre, and for the heiress who buys it, despite local talk of a curse and an old woman who keeps warning them to leave. One of Christie's darkest late novels, and far closer to gothic suspense than a cosy whodunit.

Murder Is Easy

Novel · 1939 · Luke Fitzwilliam

On a train to London, retired policeman Luke Fitzwilliam meets an elderly woman on her way to report a killer to Scotland Yard, and the next day he reads that she has been killed by a car. In the village of Wychwood under Ashe he finds gossip, suspicions of black magic, and a run of deaths nobody else seems to question.

Christie's Genuinely Supernatural Stories

Most of Christie's ghosts turn out to have a rational explanation. These two books are the exceptions, and neither has a current TV adaptation, so they're for reading by lamplight.

For Reading

The Hound of Death

Short stories · 1933 (UK)

Christie's most openly supernatural collection: twelve tales of premonitions, strange powers and a haunted house, including a séance that goes terribly wrong in The Last Séance. It also includes The Witness for the Prosecution, the one story in the book with no ghostly element.

What Readers Ask Most

Which Agatha Christie novel is set at Halloween?

Hallowe'en Party (1969), in which Poirot and Ariadne Oliver investigate a death at a children's Halloween party in the village of Woodleigh Common. David Suchet's adaptation aired in 2010 as part of Series 12 of Agatha Christie's Poirot.

Are Agatha Christie's spooky stories actually supernatural?

Usually not. In novels like The Pale Horse and The Sittaford Mystery, the séances and witchcraft have a human explanation. The exceptions are the short-story collections The Hound of Death and The Mysterious Mr Quin.

Is A Haunting in Venice based on Hallowe'en Party?

Loosely. Kenneth Branagh's 2023 film takes its starting point from Hallowe'en Party but moves the story to Venice. For a version closer to the novel, the 2010 David Suchet episode keeps the English village setting.

Where can I watch these Agatha Christie adaptations in the US?

The Poirot episodes and the 2023 Murder Is Easy are on BritBox. The Marple versions of The Pale Horse, The Sittaford Mystery and Endless Night, plus the 2015 And Then There Were None, are on Acorn TV. The 2020 BBC version of The Pale Horse streams on Prime Video. See Where to Watch for links.

Which spooky Christie book should I read first?

Hallowe'en Party if you want Poirot and a proper Halloween setting. And Then There Were None if you want the most suspense, and The Pale Horse if you want witches.
