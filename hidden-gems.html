Hidden Gems

Beyond the household names lies some genuinely excellent television. These thirteen shows deserve far bigger audiences than they have — the quietly beautiful, the cancelled-too-soon, and the quality classics hiding in plain sight.

Already know these? Try the harder challenge in So You Think You've Seen It All? Or if a favourite quietly disappeared, see Now That It's Ended.

The Detectorists

2014–2017 · BBC Four · Acorn TV

Mackenzie Crook's gentle comedy-drama about metal detectorists in rural Essex is one of the most quietly beautiful British shows around — two series of pure television treasure.

The Bletchley Circle

The Bletchley Circle

2012–2014 · ITV · BritBox

Former WWII codebreakers use their unique skills to solve post-war murders in 1950s London — a brilliant, underappreciated gem that deserves far more recognition than it gets.

Jonathan Creek

Jonathan Creek

1997–2016 · BBC One · BritBox

A reclusive magic trick designer who solves ‘impossible’ crimes from his converted windmill home, alongside an investigative journalist who keeps dragging him into cases. Endlessly clever and hugely entertaining.

Rumpole of the Bailey

Rumpole of the Bailey

1978–1992 · ITV · BritBox

Leo McKern as the irascible, cheap-wine-drinking, Wordsworth-quoting barrister Horace Rumpole, defending the Old Bailey's underdogs case after case. Witty, warm, and one of British TV's great characters.

Waking the Dead

Waking the Dead

2000–2011 · BBC One · BritBox

A cold case unit led by the volatile DCI Boyd reopens decades-old murders using new forensic techniques — darker and more psychologically complex than most crime procedurals.

Cranford

Cranford

2007–2009 · BBC One · BritBox

An all-star cast led by Judi Dench brings Elizabeth Gaskell's gentle, comic portrait of a small Victorian town to life — pure comfort viewing with real heart underneath.

Kingdom

Kingdom

2007–2009 · ITV · Acorn TV

Stephen Fry as a small-town Norfolk solicitor quietly holding his community — and his own grief — together. Gentle, warm, and quietly wonderful.

Hustle

Hustle

2004–2012 · BBC One · BritBox

A team of long-con artists work London's wealthiest marks in slick, stylish capers that let the audience in on the trick just a beat before the victim catches on. Endlessly fun.

Wire in the Blood

Wire in the Blood

2002–2008 · ITV · BritBox

Robson Green as a criminal psychologist who gets uncomfortably close inside the minds of serial killers to help police crack cases. Darker than Morse, smarter than most, and hugely underrated.

Hamish Macbeth

Hamish Macbeth

1995–1997 · BBC One · BritBox

Robert Carlyle as a laid-back Scottish Highland constable who'd honestly rather be left alone than solve another case. Charming, quirky, and utterly addictive.

Pie in the Sky

Pie in the Sky

1994–1997 · BBC One · BritBox

Richard Griffiths stars as a detective inspector who'd much rather be running his own restaurant than solving crimes — but his superiors keep dragging him back for one more case. Warm, funny, and criminally underseen.

The Tunnel

The Tunnel

2013–2017 · Sky Atlantic · PBS Masterpiece

A British-French remake of The Bridge: a body found at the exact midpoint of the Channel Tunnel forces two mismatched detectives, one from each side, into an uneasy partnership. Stephen Dillane is extraordinary in the lead.
