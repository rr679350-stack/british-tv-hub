A Ghost Story for Halloween

For over fifty years, the BBC has told Britain's best ghost stories at Christmas — antiquarian tales of cursed relics and things that shouldn't be dug up, read first by candlelight at Cambridge. It's a genuinely eerie tradition, just aimed at the wrong holiday. Here's its real history, borrowed for October.

The Cambridge Don Behind the Tradition

Few writers have shaped the British antiquarian ghost story more profoundly than one Cambridge medievalist with an unusual Christmastime habit.

Montague Rhodes James (1862–1936)
Medieval scholar · Provost of King's College, Cambridge (1905–1918) · later Provost of Eton (1918–1936)

By day, M.R. James was one of the great medievalists of his era, an expert in old manuscripts and cathedral libraries. In his spare time, he wrote ghost stories — and read them aloud to undergraduates and friends at Christmastime in his rooms at King's College. His tales follow a familiar shape: a bookish, rational man — a scholar, an antiquarian, a collector — disturbs something old and half-forgotten, and it disturbs him back. No blood, no monsters in the usual sense; just dread that builds, quietly and precisely, until it doesn't. Few writers have shaped the British antiquarian ghost story more profoundly, and generations of writers, including the ones behind this BBC tradition, have been adapting his method ever since.

The Classic Run (1971–1978)

The BBC's first attempt at bringing James to the screen, and the run that gave the strand its name and its template.

1968 · The Precursor
Whistle and I'll Come to You
Director: Jonathan Miller · Starring Michael Hordern · Based on the M.R. James story "Oh, Whistle, and I'll Come to You, My Lad"

A fussy, rationalist professor finds an old whistle on a Suffolk beach and blows it, with consequences he refuses to believe are supernatural until it's too late. Broadcast three years before the official strand began, it's usually cited as the film that proved James could work brilliantly on screen.

1971
The Stalls of Barchester
Director: Lawrence Gordon Clark · Based on the M.R. James story of the same name

The first official "A Ghost Story for Christmas," and the one that set the template: an archdeacon, a cathedral close, and carved wooden stalls that turn out to remember exactly how they were made.

1972
A Warning to the Curious
Director: Lawrence Gordon Clark · Based on the M.R. James story of the same name

An amateur archaeologist digs up one of three buried Anglo-Saxon crowns said to protect England from invasion — and finds out why the other two are still in the ground. Widely regarded as one of the strongest entries in the entire run.

1973
Lost Hearts
Director: Lawrence Gordon Clark · Based on the M.R. James story of the same name

A young orphan comes to live with his elderly cousin in a remote country house, unaware of the alchemical experiments — and the previous wards — that came before him.

1974
The Treasure of Abbot Thomas
Director: Lawrence Gordon Clark · Based on the M.R. James story of the same name

A clergyman follows a coded trail of stained-glass clues left by a medieval abbot, convinced he's about to find buried gold rather than whatever the abbot actually left behind to guard it.

1975
The Ash-tree
Director: Lawrence Gordon Clark · Based on the M.R. James story of the same name

A new squire inherits an ancestral hall and the ash tree just outside his bedroom window — the same tree implicated in his ancestor's role in a 17th-century witch trial.

1976
The Signalman
Director: Lawrence Gordon Clark · Writer: Andrew Davies · Based on the Charles Dickens story of the same name

A break from James: a railway signalman is haunted by a spectral warning before each disaster on his stretch of line, and can't convince anyone — least of all himself — what it means.

1977
Stigma
Director: Lawrence Gordon Clark · Writer: Clive Exton · Original story

A contemporary, non-James entry: a family clears a standing stone from their garden and disturbs whatever was buried beneath it, with the strand's most visceral consequences yet.

1978
The Ice House
Director: Derek Lister · Writer: John Bowen · Original story

A health-spa guest becomes fixated on a strange couple and the ice house on the grounds in an unusually oblique, dreamlike finale to the original run.

The Mark Gatiss Revival

Decades later, the co-creator of Sherlock and Dracula brought the strand back — and has been quietly keeping it alive ever since.

2005 & 2006
A View from a Hill · Number 13
The BBC's first attempts to revive the strand, three decades after it ended

Two standalone M.R. James adaptations that tested whether audiences still had an appetite for the format. They did — it just took Mark Gatiss, a lifelong James devotee, to make the revival stick.

2010
Whistle and I'll Come to You (remake)
Starring John Hurt · A new adaptation of the same M.R. James story as the 1968 original

A looser, more emotionally raw take on the 1968 classic, built around John Hurt's performance as a man unraveling after his wife's decline into dementia — with something else in the house besides.

2013
The Tractate Middoth
Written and directed by Mark Gatiss · Based on the M.R. James story of the same name

Gatiss's first outing as writer-director for the strand: a librarian, a vindictive will, and a very particular book that a dead man does not want found.

2018
The Dead Room
Written and directed by Mark Gatiss · Starring Simon Callow · Original story

Gatiss's first original screenplay for the strand: a veteran radio horror host records one more Christmas Eve broadcast in a studio with a genuinely unsettling history.

2019
Martin's Close
Written and directed by Mark Gatiss · Starring Peter Capaldi · Based on the M.R. James story of the same name

A 17th-century murder trial, told partly as courtroom drama, in which the chief witness against the accused may not entirely be among the living.

2021
The Mezzotint
Adapted by Mark Gatiss · Based on the M.R. James story of the same name

A museum curator acquires an unremarkable engraving of a country house — except the picture keeps changing, one figure at a time, getting closer.

2022
Count Magnus
Adapted by Mark Gatiss · Based on the M.R. James story of the same name

A travel writer researching a Swedish nobleman's tomb learns, too late, exactly what Count Magnus brought back from his "Black Pilgrimage."

2023
Lot No. 249
Adapted by Mark Gatiss · Starring Kit Harington and Freddie Fox · Based on the story by Arthur Conan Doyle

Gatiss turns to Arthur Conan Doyle for this wonderfully macabre Oxford tale of Egyptology, rivalry, and a reanimated mummy.

2024
Woman of Stone
Written and directed by Mark Gatiss · Based on the E. Nesbit story "Man-Size in Marble"

Gatiss looks past James again for a rare female-led entry: a newlywed couple in a country cottage learn what the local legend about two marble knights actually means.

2025
The Room in the Tower
Written and directed by Mark Gatiss · Starring Tobias Menzies and Joanna Lumley · Based on the E.F. Benson story of the same name

The strand's most recent entry: a recurring nightmare about a room that shouldn't exist turns out to be a warning, adapted from another giant of the classic British ghost story alongside James.

Why a Christmas Tradition Works for Halloween

There's nothing to correct here — this genuinely is a Christmas tradition, tied to a very specific Victorian habit of telling ghost stories around the fire in the depths of winter. Dickens leaned on the same custom for A Christmas Carol.

But the tone travels. These aren't jump-scare horror — they're slow, atmospheric, and built around the very British fear of disturbing something that should have been left alone. That's a mood October understands just as well as December, and it's exactly the kind of eerie-without-gore viewing this site tends to recommend anyway. Consider it borrowed, not reinvented.

What Readers Ask Most

Is "A Ghost Story for Christmas" actually a Halloween tradition?

No — it's a real BBC Christmas tradition dating back to 1971, built on M.R. James's own habit of reading his ghost stories aloud at Christmastime at King's College, Cambridge. British TV Hub is simply borrowing it for October, since the tone — slow-building dread, no gore — suits Halloween viewing just as well.

Who was M.R. James?

Montague Rhodes James (1862–1936) was a medieval scholar and Provost of King's College, Cambridge, and later of Eton. He wrote antiquarian ghost stories in his spare time, and few writers have shaped the genre more profoundly — see The Origins above.

Where can I watch these in the US?

BritBox carries the anthology, though which individual entries are available can rotate.

What's the difference between the original run and the Mark Gatiss revival?

The original run (1971–1978) was made by the BBC under producer-director Lawrence Gordon Clark and wasn't exclusively M.R. James even then — it closed with adaptations of Charles Dickens and two original screenplays. The strand returned intermittently before becoming a more regular Christmas fixture under Mark Gatiss, who has written and directed every revival entry since 2013, adapting James most often but also Arthur Conan Doyle, E. Nesbit, and E.F. Benson.

Where should I start?

A Warning to the Curious (1972) is the most frequently cited standout from the original run. For the revival, Martin's Close (2019), with Peter Capaldi, is a strong, self-contained entry point.
